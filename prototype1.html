What happened

The first attempt — high hopes, low tolerances

The very first CAD-to-print iteration. I went in with rough measurements from the ESP32-C3 Zero datasheet but didn't account for printer tolerance or the realities of FDM layer thickness. The result was a part that looked OK in the slicer but fell apart almost immediately on inspection.

Print orientation was wrong, which meant the walls printed with the grain of weakness — they snapped under any meaningful pressure. Even if the walls held, the board still didn't fit inside, and the GPIO pin slots were too tight to seat properly.

And of course: no lid, no ventilation. Two of the most important features for the case, completely missed. This print was a humbling reminder that 3D design is not "draw box, print box."

🟢 Pros
  • Fast print time — only 12m 57s
  • Low filament cost — $0.09
  • Established the basic outer dimensions
  • Taught me to actually measure the board
🔴 Issues identified
  • Wrong print orientation
  • Walls too thin and snapped on handling
  • Board didn't fit — internal dimensions wrong
  • GPIO pin slots too small for header pins
  • No ventilation openings
  • No lid design at all
🛠️ Planned improvements for P2
  • Two-piece design with a removable lid
  • Increased internal tolerance for board fit
  • Better print orientation
  • Begin button-hole positioning study

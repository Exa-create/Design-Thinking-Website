The team's final design — secure, vented, professional.

Our team selected Jeremy Price's Prototype 3 as the final design after testing and evaluating all available prototypes. It offered the best combination of functionality, usability, security and ease of assembly.

The ESP32-C3 Zero fits securely and snugly inside, the top section removes easily for access to the chip, and the bottom pin section is enclosed with room for cable management while keeping connections protected. The controller-style buttons give a realistic, responsive feel.

Why this one: while my own prototype showed promise, Jeremy's design was already completed and ready for implementation, making it the most practical choice within the project timeframe — with the added benefit of being wall-mountable. I'm very happy with the outcome and proud of what our team built together.

how it evolved

Improvement log — P1 → Final

Version Issue Found Likely Cause Change Made Result
Prototype 1 Walls too thin and snapped. Wrong print orientation. Board didn't fit. Pin slots too small. No ventilation. Locking clips too fragile. Measurements not verified against the physical board. Wrong orientation in slicer. Wall thickness too low. Increased internal dimensions, thickened walls, corrected orientation, enlarged pin slots, added ventilation, redesigned lid connection. P2 produced with stronger walls, correct orientation, larger pin slots and ventilation.
Prototype 2 Bottom half too tight (needed sanding). Leg-and-hole lid failed (both parts same size). Button holes misplaced. Lid wouldn't close. Tolerances not accounted for. Leg and hole at same size — no room to connect. Button positions unchecked. Adjusted dimensions with correct tolerances. Made leg smaller than hole. Corrected button positions. Hollowed top half further. P3 bottom half fit perfectly. Leg-and-hole worked. Pins protruded correctly. Only top recess needed more depth.
Final Design P3 top interior recess needed more clearance to fully close. A final design had to be chosen within the timeframe. Internal recess not deep enough, contacting the top of the board. Deadlines limited further redesign. Team reviewed all prototypes and selected Jeremy Price's Prototype 3 — secure fit, ventilation, accessible buttons/pins, removable lid, wall-mounting, strong enclosure. Met all project requirements. Board fits securely, ports/pins/buttons accessible, ventilation included, durable and professionally finished.

Conclusion

The final ESP32-C3 Zero case meets all of the project brief requirements. The board fits securely with correct clearance, the USB-C port and GPIO pins remain accessible, the buttons operate with a comfortable controller-like feel, and ventilation holes assist airflow around the chip. The enclosure is strong enough for classroom handling, printable on classroom equipment, and finished to a professional standard. Selecting Jeremy Price's Prototype 3 allowed the team to deliver a practical, secure and user-friendly product within the project timeframe, while leaving room to develop my own prototype further in the future. I am very happy with the final outcome and proud of the work our team completed together.
